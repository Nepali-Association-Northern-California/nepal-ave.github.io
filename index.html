About the Initiative

NANC & the Nepali Community Center

The Nepali Association of Northern California (NANC) is a nonprofit organization representing Nepali immigrants and Nepali-Americans across the Bay Area and Northern California.

In February 2025, NANC acquired the former Davis Chapel CME Church at 369 Chesley Avenue — a historic building that served Richmond's African American community for decades — and established the Nepali Community Center: the first permanent home for the Nepali diaspora in Richmond.

The center hosts Nepali language classes, cultural festivals including Dashain and Tihar, music and dance programs, youth activities, and social services for newly arrived Nepali families.

NANC has advocated for the civic rights and community representation of Nepali people across Northern California for years, building partnerships with Bay Area cities, institutions, and multicultural organizations.

The Nepal Avenue Initiative builds on this foundation — making our community's presence visible and permanent in the geography of Richmond itself.

The Case for the Name

Why Nepal Avenue?

The name Nepal was chosen with deliberate care — not just for what it says, but for everything it represents to every Nepali person.

01

Unity Above All

Nepal is the shared national identity of all Nepali people — Sherpa, Newari, Tamang, Gurung, Rai, Brahmin, Magar, Tharu, and every other ethnic community. No mountain, city, or festival name could represent all of us equally. Nepal does.

02

Permanent Preservation

A street name is forever. Nepal Avenue will appear on Google Maps, Apple Maps, GPS systems, postal addresses, and city archives — ensuring Nepali heritage is embedded in Richmond's geography for generations to come.

03

American Tradition

Naming streets after countries to honor immigrant communities is a well-established American tradition. Cities across the United States have streets named after the homelands of their newest neighbors. Nepal Avenue joins that proud tradition.

04

Emergency Safety

"Nepal" is clear, unambiguous, and easy to spell — ensuring that Richmond Police and Fire dispatch can locate addresses on Nepal Avenue quickly and safely. No confusion. No risk.

05

Community Identity

For Nepali families in Richmond, Nepal Avenue is a symbol of belonging — a declaration that this community has planted roots, built something real, and earned its place in the civic landscape of the city.

06

Richmond's Diversity

Richmond is one of California's most ethnically diverse cities. Nepal Avenue reinforces the city's commitment to celebrating the full spectrum of its residents' cultural identities — consistent with Richmond's own values and renaming policy.

Historical Context

The Story of Chesley Avenue

Understanding where this street came from helps explain why renaming it is the right next chapter.

1823

Rancho San Pablo

The land that would become Richmond was part of Rancho San Pablo, granted to Don Francisco Castro under Mexican land grant. This was the foundation of the city's territorial history before American settlement.

1905

Richmond is Incorporated

As the city developed, streets were named after early Anglo-American settlers, landowners, and developers who subdivided and sold land. "Chesley" — an Anglo-Saxon habitational surname derived from Old English for "meadow settlement" — followed this pattern, with no documented connection to the current community.

1940s

North Richmond Transforms

Tens of thousands of African American workers arrived from the American South to work in Kaiser Shipyards during World War II, dramatically reshaping North Richmond's demographics and building a vibrant Black community along Chesley Avenue and beyond.

1959

Davis Chapel CME Church Established

The Davis Chapel Christian Methodist Episcopal Church at 369 Chesley Avenue became a spiritual and social anchor for the Black community of North Richmond — a place of worship, fellowship, and mutual aid for decades.

Feb 2025

NANC Acquires the Building

In February 2025, NANC acquired 369 Chesley Avenue and established the Nepali Community Center — the first permanent institutional home for the Nepali diaspora in Richmond. The building continues its legacy of serving a community seeking belonging, dignity, and cultural continuity in Richmond.

2026–27

Nepal Avenue — The Next Chapter

NANC formally proposes renaming Chesley Avenue to Nepal Avenue. The street's name has no documented connection to its current residents or the community it serves. Nepal Avenue honestly and proudly reflects who calls this neighborhood home in the 21st century.

Our Home

The Nepali Community Center

The Nepali Community Center at 369 Chesley Avenue is more than a building — it is the physical embodiment of our mission. We honor the legacy of Davis Chapel CME Church and the community it served, and commit to continuing that tradition of service and welcome.

The center is open to all Richmond residents. We host cultural events, Nepali language and cooking classes, youth programs, Dashain and Tihar festivals, music performances, and social services for newly arrived Nepali families. All are welcome.

City of Richmond Official Process

How the Renaming Works

NANC is working through the City of Richmond's official street renaming process — four stages, full transparency.

1

Nomination

NANC submits a formal nomination package to the City of Richmond Recreation & Parks Director, including petition signatures, historical research, and emergency services clearance.

2

Commission Review

The Recreation & Parks Commission reviews the nomination, hears public comment, and issues a recommendation to City Council.

3

City Council Vote

The Richmond City Council votes on the renaming. Community turnout and letters of support make a critical difference at this stage.

4

Installation & Celebration

New street signs are installed by the City's Public Works Department. NANC hosts a public Nepal Avenue unveiling ceremony open to all.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I have to change my address on all my documents?

When the City officially renames a street, they provide a transition period of several months. The US Postal Service continues delivering mail to both the old and new address during this period. You'll receive official guidance on updating records — the process is simpler than most people expect.

Why change a name that has been there for over 100 years?

"Chesley" is an Anglo-Saxon habitational surname with no documented connection to any person, family, or community associated with this street. Richmond has renamed streets before — including unanimously in 2024. Names should reflect the community that lives there today.

Will this affect my property value?

Research on street renamings does not show a consistent negative effect on property values. Streets with distinctive cultural identities can actually attract increased community investment and visibility. The cost of new street signs is covered by NANC, not property owners.

Can we start numbering at 1 Nepal Ave for the Community Center?

Address renumbering is a separate and complex process governed by the city's grid system, requiring all 40+ property owners to update legal documents. NANC's recommendation is to focus on the street renaming — 369 Nepal Ave, Richmond CA is already a powerful and historic address.

What happens if not enough residents sign the petition?

The level of petition support is the single biggest factor in the City's decision. That's why NANC is conducting thorough door-to-door canvassing across all four canvassing zones on Chesley Avenue and why your participation matters so much.

Who is leading this initiative?

The Nepal Avenue Initiative is led by the NANC Board of Directors in coalition with Nepali community leaders across the Bay Area. We are a nonprofit organization with deep roots in the Northern California Nepali community. All costs are covered by NANC — no burden falls on the City or residents.

What is the total cost of this initiative?

The estimated total cost is $2,000 to $7,000, covering street sign fabrication and installation, petition materials, outreach, and the public unveiling ceremony. NANC is fully fundraising this amount within the community — there is no cost to the City of Richmond.

How does this honor Davis Chapel CME Church's legacy?

NANC deeply respects the African American community that Davis Chapel CME Church served at 369 Chesley for decades. The Nepali Community Center continues that building's tradition of serving a community seeking belonging, dignity, and cultural continuity in Richmond. We see ourselves as custodians of that legacy.
